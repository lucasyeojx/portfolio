Indonesia rates · 2s10s steepener · Entry 23 Oct 2026 · Horizon: BI meeting 15–16 Dec

Indonesian government bond 2s10s steepener on a rising governance risk premium

Open Paper trade, duration-neutral
Entry56bp
Target80bp
Stop40bp
Carry~0
Planned R:R1.5 : 1
2Y : 10Y size3.8 : 1
Thesis

Long end (main driver): MSCI's proposal to move Indonesia to Standalone status, President Prabowo's defiant response and continued foreign outflows should push 10Y yields higher as investors demand a higher risk premium to hold Indonesian duration.

Front end: Bank Indonesia is cutting reserve requirements from 1 November instead of hiking, which keeps 2Y yields contained. The deposit facility floor, raised to 5.00%, limits how far the 2Y can fall.

Why it's mispriced: at 56bp, 2s10s remains well below early-2026 levels (110–150bp), when BI was last not tightening.

What was priced

OIS traded at 5.56% (1M) and 5.73% (3M) on 22 October, so no hike was priced against the 5.75% policy rate. The 1M rate sat about 45bp above the overnight rate, so the market expected overnight rates to drift up rather than fall. 2s10s had already steepened from about 36bp (September average) to 56bp, driven by the long end.

Levels

The stop is a return to September's 31–39bp range, about five times the daily standard deviation of spread changes (3.2bp). The target is roughly a 1.25-standard-deviation move over two months; I accept a lower reward-to-risk for a higher hit rate.

Catalysts
  • Fed, 27–28 Oct and December: hawkish outcomes add long-end pressure
  • Reserve-requirement cuts, 1 Nov and 1 Dec: overnight rates staying near 5.0–5.2%
  • BI October reserves (~7 Nov): a decline signals a costly rupiah defence
  • BI meetings, 17–18 Nov and 15–16 Dec: holds with no hike signal
  • Q3 balance of payments (~20 Nov): deficit not narrowing
Invalidation
  • BI steps up purchases of long-tenor bonds, which it pairs with FX intervention
  • The 10Y falls while the rupiah is still weak
  • 1M OIS rises above 5.75%, or BI signals a hike
  • Overnight rates rise toward 5.75% after 1 November
  • A free-float rule is enacted, or foreign bond inflows resume
  • 2s10s closes below 40bp

Long USD/INR on India's balance-of-payments squeeze

Stopped out · −1.0R Spot, no derivatives
Entry96.70
Target100.00
Stop96.00
Carry~0.30
Planned R:R~3 : 1
Result−1.0R
Thesis

India faced a squeeze on both sides of its balance of payments. Oil above $100 widened the goods deficit, with a lag as import contracts repriced, while portfolio outflows driven by US yields meant the deficit was financed with volatile capital. The RBI's "orderly adjustment" stance implied fewer hikes than the 100–150bp priced in swaps, removing carry support, so USD/INR should rise faster than forwards implied.

What was priced

A gradual, RBI-managed depreciation: 3-month forward at 97.70 (~4.3% annualised), 1-year OIS at 6.26%, Brent in steep backwardation, 3-month implied vol 7.5% against realised ~3%, and MUFG at 97.5 by Q3 2027.

Catalysts
  • India September CPI and trade data (mid-Oct)
  • US September CPI; Fed meeting 27–28 Oct
  • Weekly RBI reserves and daily portfolio flows
  • RBI December meeting (horizon)
Invalidation
  • Brent falls sharply on a Hormuz reopening
  • RBI escalation: a 50bp hike, heavy intervention or curbs on dollar buying
  • Reserves stabilise or foreign portfolio inflows resume
  • USD/INR closes below 96.00

What happened

On Saturday 10 October the RBI began selling dollars directly to the three state oil companies, removing an estimated $10–12bn a month of spot demand, and tightened FX derivative rules. On Monday the rupee rallied about 1% to 95.77, its best day since April. The mechanism I traded (oil-driven dollar demand) was exactly the flow the central bank took off-market.

The RBI's "calibrated tightening" and the rupee

The RBI's 25bp hike to 5.50% on 7 October, its first in four years, was fully priced. The news was the guidance. I traced the rupee's weakness to two pressures at once: oil above $100 and portfolio outflows driven by US yields, with the real effective exchange rate already down from 108 to 91. Sep 2026 Weekly note

SNB holds at 0%: the franc's weakness is imported

The 24 September hold was expected by every surveyed economist. What changed was the SNB dropping the heightened intervention language it added in March and revising inflation up, a signal that it accepts a weaker franc as easing. I tied the franc's ~3% trade-weighted decline to the repricing of foreign rate paths rather than to the hold itself.
