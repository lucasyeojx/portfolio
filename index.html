Process

How an idea becomes a trade

Every idea goes through the same eight steps, in order. An idea that fails a step is logged and dropped.

  1. 01Start from a changeA decision, data surprise or price shock. A level is not news.
  2. 02Map the transmissionEvent, mechanism, asset. Pick the asset with the shortest chain.
  3. 03Find what's pricedSwaps, forwards, implied vol, positioning. The edge is the gap to my view.
  4. 04Name the catalystDated events that force the market toward the view.
  5. 05Choose the instrumentClean exposure, carry cost, access, risk shape.
  6. 06Set the levelsStop beyond realised-vol noise; at least 2:1 after carry.
  7. 07Run a pre-mortemThesis, timing, crowding, event risk. Observable invalidation triggers.
  8. 08Write the ticketOne page. Then log it, and review it against the outcome.

Thesis

India faced a squeeze on both sides of its balance of payments. Oil above $100 widened the goods deficit, with a lag as import contracts repriced, while portfolio outflows driven by US yields meant the deficit was financed with volatile capital. The RBI's "orderly adjustment" stance implied fewer hikes than the 100–150bp priced in swaps, removing carry support, so USD/INR should rise faster than forwards implied.

What was priced

A gradual, RBI-managed depreciation: 3-month forward at 97.70 (~4.3% annualised), 1-year OIS at 6.26%, Brent in steep backwardation, 3-month implied vol 7.5% against realised ~3%, and MUFG at 97.5 by Q3 2027.

Catalysts
  • India September CPI and trade data (mid-Oct)
  • US September CPI; Fed meeting 27–28 Oct
  • Weekly RBI reserves and daily portfolio flows
  • RBI December meeting (horizon)
Invalidation
  • Brent falls sharply on a Hormuz reopening
  • RBI escalation: a 50bp hike, heavy intervention or curbs on dollar buying
  • Reserves stabilise or foreign portfolio inflows resume
  • USD/INR closes below 96.00

What happened

On Saturday 10 October the RBI began selling dollars directly to the three state oil companies, removing an estimated $10–12bn a month of spot demand, and tightened FX derivative rules. On Monday the rupee rallied about 1% to 95.77, its best day since April. The mechanism I traded (oil-driven dollar demand) was exactly the flow the central bank took off-market. The risk was on my invalidation list; I underweighted it because the RBI used the same tool in 2013.

The RBI's "calibrated tightening" and the rupee

The RBI's 25bp hike to 5.50% on 7 October, its first in four years, was fully priced. The news was the guidance. I traced the rupee's weakness to two pressures at once: oil above $100 and portfolio outflows driven by US yields, with the real effective exchange rate already down from 108 to 91. Sep 2026 Weekly note

SNB holds at 0%: the franc's weakness is imported

The 24 September hold was expected by every surveyed economist. What changed was the SNB dropping the heightened intervention language it added in March and revising inflation up, a signal that it accepts a weaker franc as easing. I tied the franc's ~3% trade-weighted decline to the repricing of foreign rate paths rather than to the hold itself.

Oct 2026 Paper in progress

Why India's inflation lagged the oil shock

Sierra Economic Research sprint. The RBI's own estimate (+10% crude ≈ +20bp CPI) implied about 120bp of extra inflation from a ~60% oil rise, yet headline CPI barely moved from February to April. A ₹10/litre excise cut and a pump-price freeze absorbed the shock. Once the freeze ended, transport CPI rose to 5.01% and headline to 5.54% by September. The paper asks how fiscal buffers change the timing and mix of pass-through.

Oct 2026 Developing idea

China's export boom is a price cycle

September exports rose 36.3% against 25.7% expected, but chip export value rose 136% on volume up only 1%. If the boom is mostly memory-chip prices, Asian export growth can decelerate quickly when prices turn. Chip equities fell more than 14% in seven sessions; the open question is whether Asian FX has priced the same risk. Research stage, no position.

Lessons library

What the losses taught me

Drawn from post-mortems in the trade log. Each one changes how the next ticket is written.

  1. Don't start a trend trade in a managed currency at the level the central bank is defending. Check its intent, its capacity, and where it has drawn the line.
  2. Map the full policy toolkit, including market-plumbing tools such as direct dollar windows and derivative rules, and check the central bank's last crisis playbook.
  3. When implied volatility sits far above realised, the market is pricing a jump. Size smaller or use a defined-risk structure.
  4. Crowded positioning is what makes a reversal violent, not just a cap on upside.
  5. Policy can land on weekends. Cut size before weekends and known events.
